# Gateway overhead benchmark: 2026-10-17, `ec9c440`

Measured with `load/run.sh` ([load/README.md](../../load/README.md)) on 2026-10-17, from 12:45 to 13:30 UTC, at commit `ec9c440`. Target: NFR-01, gateway overhead p95 < 100 ms. Every number below was measured in this run (DoD #10). Nothing is extrapolated.

This run repeats the [266c696 benchmark](2026-10-17-266c696.md) (the "before") with the fixes for its findings merged:

- `budget_period` is locked only for Tenants with a Budget, and only for a one-statement window.
- The Npgsql pools are capped at 40 (app) and 5 (migrator, retention) in `.env.example`.
- Compose starts PostgreSQL with `max_connections=200`.
- Unrecorded 500s are counted with `error_code` `internal_error`.
- EF Core and `HttpClient` now log at Warning.

## Result

- **NFR-01 now holds up to 200 RPS in both variants, with no errors.** The setup is one Tenant, a 200 ms Mock Provider, and a 4-CPU / 4 GB Docker VM on a laptop.
  - Non-streaming overhead p95: **3.4 ms** at 50 RPS, **4.4 ms** at 100 and **13.6 ms** at 200.
  - Streaming overhead p95: **8.7 ms** at 50 RPS, **11.2 ms** at 100 and **48.9 ms** at 200.
- **At 400 RPS the stack saturates** and the target is missed. It achieves about **340 RPS** non-streaming and **274 RPS** streaming. Overhead p95 is about 2.4–2.5 s, and k6 drops 17 275 and 37 118 iterations.
  - Unlike before, there are **no errors**: the capped pool now queues callers instead of PostgreSQL refusing them.
- **The new saturation point is the database round trips per request, on a VM that has run out of CPU.**
  - At 400 RPS, PostgreSQL uses about 1.4–1.65 cores and the api about 1.2–1.4 cores (`docker stats`). otel-lgtm and Caddy take most of the remaining 4 cores.
  - The api's pool sits at 40 of 40, and DB operation p95 rises from about 1 ms to about 40 ms.
  - A mid-step `pg_stat_activity` snapshot (streaming, 400 RPS) showed **no lock waits**. About 30 sessions were `idle in transaction` inside settlement transactions (`UPDATE inference_request`, `WITH released AS (UPDATE reservation …)`, `SAVEPOINT`, `INSERT usage_record`, `INSERT outbox_event`), waiting for the api's next statement.
  - GC pauses also grew to about 30–34 s per 5-minute step, about 10 % of wall time, with 33–37 gen2 collections.
- **The outbox relay falls behind from 200 RPS.** Its lag was under 1 s at 100 RPS, 189 s at the end of the 200 RPS step, and 1 866 s by the end of the run.
  - The worker used about 1 % CPU throughout. This matches how `OutboxRelay` works: one event per transaction, in sequence.
  - Usage rollups (`usage_hourly`) therefore trail the traffic by minutes above about 150 RPS. Request success and Reservations are not affected.

## Findings

These are follow-ups. Code was not tuned for this report.

1. **Round trips in settlement.** The Settle transaction issues several statements (request update, Attempts, Usage Records, Reservation release, outbox insert, EF savepoints). Each one holds a pooled connection while it waits on the api. At about 300–340 RPS this saturates the 40-connection pool on this VM. Fewer round trips per transaction would raise the ceiling, for example batched statements without savepoints. So would a smaller per-request write set.
2. **Outbox relay throughput.** `OutboxRelay` handles one event per transaction, in sequence, and the worker is idle on CPU. It fell behind somewhere between 100 and 200 RPS. Rollups and anything else downstream of the outbox lag by minutes under sustained load above that.
3. **GC at saturation.** About 10 % of wall time was spent in GC pauses at 400 RPS, with about 100 MiB of heap and 33–37 gen2 collections per 5 min. This was not investigated: it may be a consequence of the queueing (more requests in flight at once), not a cause.

## Limitations

- **Laptop hardware.** The Docker VM has 4 CPUs and 3.8 GiB, and runs the whole stack plus `otel-lgtm`, which used 0.9–2.3 GiB and briefly about 1 core. At 400 RPS the VM is the limit, so a larger machine or a separate database host would move the ceiling. k6 ran on the macOS host, through Docker Desktop's port forwarder.
- **Single api replica.** The standard workload uses one Tenant with no limits, so Budget enforcement and the Valkey limit path are not exercised (Budget locking only applies to Tenants with a Budget now).
- **Sequential steps on one stack.** The outbox backlog from the non-streaming 200 and 400 RPS steps was still being relayed during the streaming steps, and that added PostgreSQL work to them.
- **Not measured:** Npgsql pool wait time (no instrument) and Valkey on the request path. The Prometheus percentiles are interpolated within histogram buckets; the DB percentiles are exact to 1 ms.
- **Trace sampling** was 1.0. A production deployment would likely sample traces, which lowers the per-request cost. No such tuning was applied.
- **Mock Provider.** The mock answers after exactly 200 ms with a tiny body.
